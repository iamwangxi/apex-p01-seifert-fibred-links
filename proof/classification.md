## 2.1. Ambient and solid-torus cases

**Lemma 2.1.** A Seifert-fibred link exterior is irreducible. The nontrivial non-split cases have incompressible boundary, and each nondisk incompressible spanning component is essential. The solid-torus case is the unknot, has one spanning vertex, and has $`k\ne0`$ for every Seifert fibering.

**Proof.**

A Seifert-fibred link exterior is irreducible: Hatcher Proposition 1.13, printed pp.23–24, has only closed exceptional manifolds. Its boundary is nonempty. A splitting sphere would bound no ball in the exterior, so such a link is automatically non-split.

For any non-split link other than the unknot, the boundary tori are incompressible. To see the exception directly, let a compressing disk for a boundary torus have essential primitive boundary $`a\mu_i+b\lambda_i`$. Its image in $`H_1(E)`$ is zero, forcing $`a=0`$ and $`b=\pm1`$, and forcing the cross-linking coefficients to vanish. Attaching the collar annulus to $`L_i`$ produces a disk bounded by $`L_i`$, disjoint from all other components. A small ball neighborhood of that disk separates this unknotted component from the others. This contradicts non-splitness when $`n>1`$, and makes the knot an unknot when $`n=1`$. The same disk argument excludes disk components of spanning surfaces in the nontrivial non-split case.

Every nondisk incompressible spanning component is therefore essential in Hatcher's sense. Hatcher Lemma 1.11 (printed p.20) leaves either boundary-incompressibility or a boundary-parallel annulus. That annulus has two boundary curves on the same ambient torus, forbidden by the one-curve-per-torus convention. This is why Hatcher Proposition 1.12 is applicable; incompressibility alone was not its hypothesis.

If $`E`$ is a solid torus, there is one link component and it is the unknot: a meridian disk of $`E`$ extends to a disk bounded by that component. Hatcher Lemma 1.11 excludes the annulus exception for a one-boundary spanning surface, and Hatcher Lemma 1.10 (printed pp.19–20) says the remaining essential surface is its meridian disk. Hence $`X(U)`$ is a point. Hatcher uses a convention excluding disks from the word “incompressible”; our convention includes spanning disks, and Hatcher's essential-disk clause covers this case.

Every Seifert fibering of a solid torus is a model fibering (Hatcher Corollary 1.15, printed p.25). A regular fibre represents $`\pm q`$ times its core for some multiplicity $`q\ge1`$ (Hatcher, pp. 17–18). Since $`\phi`$ is a primitive generator for the unknot exterior, $`k=\phi(h)=\pm q\ne0`$. The meridian disk is horizontal. There is consequently no omitted solid-torus $`k=0`$ case. PS's boundary-irreducible-manifold hypothesis is not applied to the unknot.

An incompressible spanning surface exists. Start with a spanning surface from Seifert's construction and compress in the interior, discarding closed descendants. A compression never joins two boundary-bearing components. The discarded closed pieces do not change the spanning class: $`H_2(E)\to H_2(E,\partial E)`$ is zero, since absolute second homology is generated by boundary tori. For termination use

```math
q(F)=\frac{n-\chi(F)}2=\sum_C g(C)+n-b_0(F)\in\mathbb N.
```

A nonseparating compression, or a separating compression with boundary on both sides, decreases $`q`$ by one. If one side is closed, its genus is at least one, or the compressing circle would be inessential; discarding it decreases $`q`$ by that genus. An innermost intersection argument supplies a compressing disk whose interior misses the whole system whenever a component is compressible. Thus the process terminates with a reduced componentwise incompressible spanning surface. ∎


## 2.2. The full filling classification

**Theorem 2.2.** A link with Seifert-fibred exterior is a union of fibres of a weighted fibering of $`S^3`$, or a keychain, with arbitrary component orientations. Conversely both constructions have Seifert-fibred exteriors.

For completeness the following classification is derived from meridional filling; it is not attributed to the surface-classification proposition. Up to homeomorphism of oriented pairs, allowing the component orientations to be assigned arbitrarily, they are precisely the union of these two constructions:

**Fibre-union construction.** A finite nonempty collection of fibres in a Seifert fibering of $`S^3`$. Equivalently, choose coprime nonzero integers $`p,q`$; take finitely many regular orbits of
   
```math
(z_1,z_2)\longmapsto(e^{ipt}z_1,e^{iqt}z_2),\qquad (z_1,z_2)\in S^3\subset\mathbb C^2,
```

Optionally include either or both coordinate-core circles. Regular orbits are parallel $`(p,q)`$ torus curves; the core circles may be exceptional or regular. This includes torus knots/links, their core-axis additions, and the elementary unknot/Hopf cases.
**Keychain construction.** one unknotted central component together with $`n-1`$ mutually unlinked meridian circles, each linked once with the central component, in the standard product arrangement. Its exterior is $`S^1\times\Sigma_{0,n}`$. For $`n=1`$ this is the unknot and for $`n=2`$ a Hopf link. The two constructions need not be disjoint.

In particular “a union of fibres of a fibering of $`S^3`$” alone is not a complete classification: the meridian-equals-fibre filling case must be included.

**Derivation.** An orientation-reversing simple base loop, avoiding cone points, would lift to an embedded Klein bottle (Hatcher, printed p.32). No closed nonorientable surface embeds in $`S^3`$: a one-sided loop would intersect it once modulo two, contradicting $`H_1(S^3;\mathbb Z/2)=0`$. Thus the base is orientable. A nonseparating base loop would lift to a torus whose complement in $`E`$ is connected, whereas every embedded torus in $`S^3`$ separates (also Hatcher, printed p.14). The base therefore has genus zero and $`n`$ boundary circles.

Remove the exceptional-fibre neighborhoods and choose a section of the resulting product circle bundle. With $`h`$ the oriented regular fibre, exceptional loops $`x_j`$, and oriented boundary section loops $`d_i`$, van Kampen gives


```math
\pi_1(E)=\left\langle h,x_1,\ldots,x_s,d_1,\ldots,d_n\,\middle|\,
 h\text{ central},\quad x_j^{q_j}h^{c_j}=1,\quad
 x_1\cdots x_s d_1\cdots d_n=1\right\rangle.
```


Any section integer in the last relation can be absorbed into a $`d_i`$, since the base has boundary. Here $`q_j\ge2`$ and $`\gcd(c_j,q_j)=1`$. The meridian slope on $`T_i`$ is


```math
\mu_i=d_i^{a_i}h^{b_i},\qquad \gcd(a_i,b_i)=1.
```


These coordinates follow directly from Hatcher's model and section construction, printed pp.32–35. The exponent $`|a_i|`$ is the meridian's intersection with the regular fibre, not its intersection with the spanning longitude.

**No fibre-meridian.** If every $`a_i\ne0`$, the fibering extends across every original meridional solid-torus filling, with core multiplicity $`|a_i|`$ (Hatcher, printed p.33). The filled manifold is the original $`S^3`$, and its base is $`S^2`$. Its orbifold group is the quotient of $`\pi_1(S^3)`$ by the fibre, and hence trivial. A sphere with three or more cone points has nontrivial orbifold group: killing all but three cone generators surjects onto


```math
\langle u,v,w\mid u^a=v^b=w^c=uvw=1\rangle,
\qquad a,b,c\ge2.
```


This triangle group has a nontrivial rotation representation from reflections in a triangle of angles $`\pi/a,\pi/b,\pi/c`$, on the sphere, Euclidean plane, or hyperbolic plane according as the angle sum is greater than, equal to, or less than $`\pi`$. The product of two adjacent reflections has the indicated order and is not the identity; the three rotation generators multiply to one. Thus there are at most two genuine cone points. With two orders their orbifold group is $`\mathbb Z/\gcd(a,b)`$, so the orders are coprime.

Split the base sphere into two disks containing at most one cone point each. Their preimages are model-fibred solid tori, by the same model construction (Hatcher, printed p.41). In their common torus the meridians intersect once: van Kampen, or its first-homology matrix, gives intersection magnitude one because the union is $`S^3`$. Choose them as the standard Heegaard basis. The common regular-fibre slope is primitive and is nonmeridional on both sides, so it has coprime nonzero coordinates $`p,q`$. The solid-torus model on either side is determined by this slope; it is the weighted fibering displayed above. Original filling cores are fibres, proving the fibre-union construction in this case. The reduction to the standard $`S^3`$ gluing is the elementary lens-space calculation in Hatcher, printed pp.40–41.

**At least one fibre-meridian.** Let $`z`$ be the number of $`a_i=0`$. Primitivity makes the corresponding meridian $`h^{\pm1}`$, so the filled group kills $`h`$. Solve the final relation of the presentation for one such unrestricted $`d_i`$. The filled fundamental group is then the free product


```math
\left(\mathop{*}_{j=1}^s\mathbb Z/q_j\right)
 *\left(\mathop{*}_{a_i\ne0}\mathbb Z/|a_i|\right)
 * F_{z-1}.
```


Triviality of $`\pi_1(S^3)`$ forces $`z=1`$, $`s=0`$, and $`|a_i|=1`$ on every other boundary. Thus $`E=S^1\times\Sigma_{0,n}`$. Change the section along arcs from the distinguished boundary to the others to remove their integers $`b_i`$; the compensating change on the distinguished boundary does not change its fibre meridian. This is the explicit section-twist move of Hatcher, printed p.34. Filling all other boundaries along the resulting $`d_i`$ gives $`S^1\times D^2`$, with $`n-1`$ standard parallel product cores. Filling its remaining boundary along $`h`$ makes $`S^3`$. The final core is an unknot, and those product cores are its standard mutually unlinked meridians. This is precisely the keychain construction. Also $`\phi(h)=\pm1`$ on its distinguished meridian, so this fibering is in the $`k\ne0`$ branch.

Conversely deleting fibre neighborhoods in the fibre-union construction preserves a Seifert fibering. In the keychain construction, the complement of the central unknot is $`D^2\times S^1`$, and deleting neighborhoods of the standard product cores leaves $`\Sigma_{0,n}\times S^1`$. This proves both directions of the classification. It includes the solid-torus case rather than assuming boundary-irreducibility there. No classification of arbitrary graph-link exteriors or multiple Seifert JSJ pieces is asserted. ∎

## 2.3. The nonzero branch is a point

**Lemma 2.3.** If $`k\ne0`$, every incompressible spanning surface is connected and horizontal; its cut exterior is a product and it is a spanning fibre.

**Proof.** Apart from the solid-torus case, Lemma 2.1 makes every component essential. On each boundary torus the primitive kernel slope $`\gamma_i`$ and a fibre satisfy

```math
\lvert\gamma_i\mathbin{\cdot}h_i\rvert=\lvert k\rvert.
```

To see this, expand the fibre in the basis $`(\mu_i,\gamma_i)`$ and apply $`\phi`$; its meridional coefficient is $`\pm k`$. Hatcher Proposition 1.12 makes each component vertical or horizontal. Nonzero intersection excludes vertical components. Every connected horizontal component finitely covers the entire base and meets every boundary torus. Since the spanning convention permits only one curve per torus, there is exactly one component. Lemma 1.1 makes its whole complement connected. Hatcher's horizontal-surface argument, p. 23, then gives $`E\mathbin{\|}F=F\times I`$, rather than a separating semi-bundle. Gluing the horizontal faces makes a surface bundle in the specified class $`\mathrm{PD}(\phi)`$. ∎

**Lemma 2.4 (all-incompressible fibred point).** A non-split link fibred in its oriented spanning class has exactly one incompressible spanning vertex.

**Proof.**

Let $`J`$ be non-split and fibred, with connected spanning fibre $`F`$. Any incompressible spanning surface disjoint from $`F`$ lies in $`E(J)\mathbin{\|}F=F\times I`$. Straighten its single boundary curve in every vertical annulus to the middle curve. Choose disjoint proper arcs cutting $`F`$ into a disk, and their vertical rectangles. Inessential intersection circles with these disks are removed by incompressibility and irreducibility. Each rectangle has exactly two boundary intersections and hence exactly one trace arc. Straighten these arcs successively. Cutting along them leaves an incompressible system in a ball, with exactly the boundary of the horizontal fibre disk. Cutting a surface along proper arcs injects the groups of its pieces into the original group; thus each piece has trivial group and is a disk. There is one boundary circle and hence one disk, isotopic to the horizontal disk. Gluing back proves the original system isotopic to $`F`$.

Kakimizu Theorem A (printed p.226) says the original geometric incompressible complex is connected. The fibre vertex has no distinct geometric neighbor by the product argument, so it is its only vertex. This uses Kakimizu's **connectivity theorem**; no disconnected-surface metric equivalence is needed. Therefore $`X(J)`$ is a point. The unknot is covered by Lemma 2.1. This proves the all-incompressible point assertion, not merely the minimum-norm assertion. ∎

## 2.4. The exhaustive zero branch

**Theorem 2.5.** If $`k=0`$, all actual filling cores are regular. The link consists of $`2m`$ regular orbits of a weighted $`S^3`$ circle action, with $`m`$ in each orientation and all genuine exceptional orbits retained in the exterior. There are zero, one, or two retained cones; in the last case their orders are coprime. Every such balanced construction occurs.

**Proof.**

Use the Seifert presentation of Theorem 2.2, with $`h`$ the oriented regular fibre, actual-boundary section generators $`d_i`$, retained cone generators $`x_j`$ and orders $`q_j\ge2`$:

```math
\pi_1(E)=\left\langle h,x_1,\ldots,x_s,d_1,\ldots,d_n\ \middle|
 h\text{ central},\quad x_j^{q_j}h^{c_j}=1,\quad
 x_1\cdots x_s d_1\cdots d_n=1\right\rangle,
\qquad \mu_i=d_i^{a_i}h^{b_i}.
```

The integers in each boundary slope are coprime. Assume $`k=\phi(h)=0`$. Applying $`\phi`$ gives

```math
q_j\phi(x_j)=0,\qquad 1=a_i\phi(d_i).
```

Consequently

```math
\phi(x_j)=0,\qquad a_i=\phi(d_i)=\epsilon_i\in\{1,-1\},
\qquad \sum_i\epsilon_i=0.
```

Every original meridional filling is transverse to the regular fibre with intersection one. Thus the fibering extends to the original $`S^3`$, and **every actual link component is a regular filling core**. A genuine removed exceptional fibre, with meridional intersection greater than one, is impossible. The fibre-meridian/keychain alternative of Theorem 2.2 has $`\phi(h)=\pm1`$ and is also impossible here. There is no $`k=0`$ solid-torus case, since its regular fibre is a nonzero multiple of the core in $`H_1`$.

Theorem 2.2 therefore leaves exactly finitely many regular orbits in a weighted $`S^3`$ circle action

```math
(z_1,z_2)\longmapsto(e^{ipt}z_1,e^{iqt}z_2),\qquad
p,q\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\},\quad \gcd(p,q)=1.
```

No genuine exceptional orbit has been removed. The actual number is $`n=2m`$, $`m\ge1`$, with $`m`$ components in each fibre orientation. The retained cone orders are the entries greater than one among $`\lvert p\rvert,\lvert q\rvert`$. There are at most two, and if there are two they are coprime. Both signs of the weights, all placements of the labelled regular orbits and every balanced label assignment are included.

The exhaustive alternatives are:

- No retained cone: $`\lvert p\rvert=\lvert q\rvert=1`$, the balanced Hopf fibres, with every $`2m`$ actual boundary count.
- One retained cone of arbitrary order at least two: exactly one absolute weight is one, again with every $`2m`$ actual boundary count.
- Two retained cones of arbitrary coprime orders at least two: both absolute weights exceed one, including the two-actual-boundary case $`m=1`$.

When the regular orbits are placed on one standard torus, the underlying link is $`T(2mp,2mq)`$. The argument below uses the base with labelled regular holes, so it does not depend on that convenient placement.

Conversely every listed case is realised by that circle action with $`2m`$ regular orbits, $`m`$ in each orientation. Its fibre has $`k=pq\sum_i\epsilon_i=0`$ and its nonzero pairwise linking makes it non-split. Thus the list is necessary and sufficient, not just a list of examples. ∎

## 2.5. Slopes, fibre order and the pullback cover

For coherent fibre orientations, pairwise linking is $`pq`$: push one of two parallel torus curves into a Heegaard solid torus, where it represents $`q`$ times its core, while the other curve links that core $`p`$ times. This also gives the torus framing, so the regular fibre on $`T_i`$ is $`h_i=\lambda_i^H+pq\mu_i^H`$. In actual orientations $`\mu_i=\epsilon_i\mu_i^H`$, $`\lambda_i=\epsilon_i\lambda_i^H`$. Since the signs balance,

```math
s_i=\sum_{j\ne i}\mathrm{lk}(L_i,L_j)=-pq,\qquad
\gamma_i=\lambda_i-s_i\mu_i=\lambda_i+pq\mu_i=\epsilon_i h_i.
```

The total-link slope $`\gamma_i`$ is forced: the coefficient of $`[\mu_i]`$ in the zero boundary class $`\sum_i(\lambda_i+b_i\mu_i)`$ is $`s_i+b_i`$. Thus $`b_i=pq`$. In particular $`\phi(\lambda_i)=-pq`$, $`\phi(\gamma_i)=0`$, and $`\lvert\mu_i\mathbin{\cdot}h_i\rvert=1`$. Also

```math
[h]=pq\sum_i[\mu_i^H]\ne0\quad\text{in }H_1(E;\mathbb Z),
```

so the fibre has infinite order, even though $`\phi(h)=0`$.

Let $`B`$ be the underlying planar orbifold with actual circles $`C_1,\ldots,C_n`$ and cone points $`o_1,\ldots,o_s`$. Set $`P=B\setminus\bigcup_j\mathrm{int}U_j`$, with unused auxiliary circles $`A_j=\partial U_j`$. The orbifold group is

```math
\Gamma=\left\langle x_1,\ldots,x_s,d_1,\ldots,d_n\ \middle|
x_j^{q_j}=1,\quad x_1\cdots x_s d_1\cdots d_n=1\right\rangle
\cong C_{q_1}*\cdots*C_{q_s}*F_{n-1}.
```

The map $`\phi`$ kills both the fibre and every cone generator, and hence factors through the **ordinary underlying** base:

```math
\phi=p^*\psi,\qquad \psi(d_i)=\epsilon_i,
\qquad \psi(x_j)=0.
```

Its cyclic cover is the pullback of the ordinary $`\psi`$ cover of $`B`$. This does not make the exterior a circle product at a cone. The endpoint demands are one at every $`C_i`$ and zero at every $`A_j`$; cone orders are not horizontal endpoint multiplicities.
