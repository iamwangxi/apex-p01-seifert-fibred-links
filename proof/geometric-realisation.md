# 10. Geometric simplices for a Seifert-fibred exterior

[Overview](../README.md) · [Objects](objects.md) · [Decoder](matching-carrier.md) · [Retraction](geometric-retraction.md)

The geometric complex $`Y=IS(L)`$ is defined in Section 1. In the balanced branch, write $`P`$ for the ordinary planar base obtained by deleting fixed cone neighbourhoods. All auxiliary circles remain marked and unused. Theorem 4.1 identifies ordinary three-dimensional vertices with joint proper multiarc classes. Theorem 5.3 computes their full-complement class distance; Lemma 5.4 excludes essential crossings in cyclic simplices. Theorem 6.1 supplies the continuous decoder on every finite compatible support, its exact edge expectation, zero-face agreement and recovery of every point of a true cyclic simplex. Theorem 7.1 supplies cyclic contractibility. These results are proved in this repository, with their external inputs declared in the bibliography.

For nonzero fibre evaluation or a solid-torus exterior, Lemmas 2.1, 2.3 and 2.4 show that the entire vertex set is a singleton, so $`X=Y`$ is a point. When $`k=0,n=2`$ and there are zero or one retained cones, the seam is unique by Section 3.5. The four-holed case with two cones is retained below. In all other cases choose one hyperbolic metric with geodesic boundary on $`P`$ and use perpendicular geodesics for distinct free-endpoint arc classes. Pairwise compatible classes then have simultaneously disjoint canonical representatives. Repeated occurrences are allocated separately. The free-endpoint and half-bigon conventions are those of Farb–Margalit, printed pp. 33–36.

## 10.1. Simultaneous verticalisation with multiplicities


**Lemma 10.1 (finite annulus systems).** In a $`k=0`$ exterior above, any finite disjoint family of incompressible spanning surfaces can be carried by **one** ambient isotopy to a disjoint union of vertical annuli. All original annulus occurrences, their grouping into spanning surfaces and their orientations are retained. Several occurrences may be parallel. Projecting to the base gives a disjoint multiarc system with multiplicities. Its arcs can be placed outside every fixed cone neighbourhood.

**Proof.** First, every component is an annulus joining two distinct actual tori, by Section 2. The core is the infinite-order regular fibre. Such an annulus is incompressible, is not parallel into the ambient boundary, and is boundary-incompressible: an essential proper arc in an annulus joins its two boundary circles, whereas the boundary side of a boundary-compressing disk would have to lie in one ambient boundary component. This is impossible for two different tori. These assertions apply to every annulus in the disjoint union $`\mathcal A`$, even though $`\mathcal A`$ has several curves on each torus and need not have connected complement.

One must justify a finite-system version of Hatcher Proposition 1.12 rather than cite a disconnected-surface statement that was not printed. Hatcher defaults to connected surfaces on printed p.12. Here is the required extension of its proof, printed pp.20–22.

Straighten all the disjoint boundary curves simultaneously to distinct regular fibres on their tori, using collar isotopies. Remove neighbourhoods of all exceptional fibres, and a further regular fibre if necessary as in the printed proof, and cut the resulting circle bundle along a finite set of vertical annuli so that the base is a disk and the cut space is a solid torus. Choose the cutting annuli with their boundary fibres disjoint from all the actual boundary curves already straightened. Those actual curves are therefore untouched by cutting. Put the **entire finite union** $`\mathcal A`$ into the normal position used in that proof; in particular no intersection arc has an actual-boundary endpoint.

The reductions of printed p.21 remain reductions of an embedded union. An inessential intersection circle on a cutting annulus is treated innermost with respect to the whole system, using componentwise incompressibility and irreducibility. An edgemost returning intersection arc cannot end on actual boundary in this position. On the deleted-fibre side, Hatcher's edgemost move removes two intersections with the deleted core. For a compression of a cut piece, the disk-cleaning argument on that page takes an innermost circle of intersection with **all** the other cut pieces. Incompressibility supplies a disk in the original annulus containing that circle. If this disk stays in the cut solid torus it cleans the compression disk; if it crosses the cut, the same isotopy reduces the cut traces. This uses a disk on a single original component, not connectedness between different components. These are precisely the two cases described in the printed proof. The usual innermost/edgemost choices clean obstructing disks before the move; all moves are isotopies of the embedded system, extended to the ambient manifold. They do not surger, discard, join or identify original annuli. The nonnegative finite intersection counts decrease at the reduction steps, exactly as for one surface.

After these reductions, the cut pieces are boundary-parallel annuli or meridian disks, with boundaries of the vertical or horizontal types in Hatcher's proof. Disjoint essential curves of both types cannot occur on the one boundary torus of the cut solid torus. An actual boundary curve of $`\mathcal A`$ is vertical and is untouched by cutting, so the type is vertical for the entire system. Thus every cut piece is a boundary-parallel annulus of the vertical type; the meridian-disk/horizontal branch is excluded. Straighten this finite collection relative to its boundary, starting with an outermost boundary-parallel annulus and proceeding through the nesting. Equivalently, their noncrossing pairing on a meridian disk determines disjoint vertical bands. This gives the simultaneous version of the relative straightening on printed p.22. It also shows that no horizontal meridian patch remains at a removed exceptional core.

Glue back the cutting annuli. All cut-face boundary curves were fixed during the last straightening, so the isotopies glue. The resulting annuli are vertical in the original fibering. No step needed the complement of the union to be connected, no step required just one boundary curve per torus for the **union**, and no parallel annulus was removed. Component labels and orientations have been carried throughout by the ambient isotopy.

Finally the projected finite disjoint regular arcs avoid the cone points. The radial isotopies of Section 2 move this entire finite system off the prescribed cone neighbourhoods; they lift in the fibre-solid-torus charts and do not meet their exceptional cores. Thus its base arcs lie in $`P`$. ∎

**Corollary 10.2 (one support for a geometric simplex).** Every simplex of $`Y`$ has its matching vertices contained in $`\mathcal M(G)`$ for one finite graph $`G`$ of distinct compatible proper classes on $`P`$.

**Proof.** Choose simultaneous disjoint representatives in the definition of the simplex and apply Lemma 10.1 to their entire union. Project the annuli, forget multiplicities only at the level of the **set of proper classes**, and straighten those distinct classes canonically. Their representatives are still pairwise compatible. Theorem 4.1 identifies each original whole surface with the matching given by its component classes. A single matching cannot repeat an arc, since it uses each actual boundary only once. Hence all vertices belong to $`\mathcal M(G)`$. Parallel occurrences across different vertices have not changed any vertex. ∎


## 10.2. Simultaneous realisation and flagness

**Lemma 10.3 (duplicate every occurrence).** For every graph $`G`$ as above, **all** the distinct vertices $`\mathcal M(G)`$ span one simplex $`\Delta_G`$ of $`Y`$.

**Proof.** Take mutually disjoint narrow rectangles around the proper edges of $`G`$, with disjoint endpoint intervals on each actual boundary circle for distinct incident edges. This is possible because the graph consists of disjoint proper arcs before the actual circles are capped. For each edge $`e`$ allocate one separate parallel copy inside its rectangle for every matching containing $`e`$. There are only finitely many matchings. Keep these copies disjoint at their endpoints as well; a rectangle supplies the corresponding consistent order at its two ends.

For each $`M\in\mathcal M(G)`$ take the union of the inverse images of its allocated copies. It is an oriented annular spanning surface, componentwise incompressible, with the required single curve on each actual torus. Moving an edge to a close parallel copy changes neither its proper isotopy class nor the whole vertex, by Theorem 4.1. Different matchings use different allocated occurrences even when they share an edge, so these **whole** surfaces are simultaneously disjoint. They use parallel boundary fibres, not one common literal boundary curve. All rectangles avoid the unused auxiliary circles and both cone neighbourhoods. This proves the simplex assertion. ∎

There is no ordering requirement between different annuli of a spanning surface. Allocating copies of one edge does not impose a global cyclic-cover block order on the other edges. This is why Lemma 10.3 is compatible with the distance-two double-lens example; geometric disjointness does not imply cyclic adjacency.

**Proposition 10.4 (geometric simplex criterion and inclusion).** A finite collection of distinct matching vertices is a simplex of $`Y`$ if and only if the union of its component proper classes is compatible on $`P`$. Moreover $`X\subseteq Y`$ as a simplicial subcomplex, by the identity on vertices. In this Seifert family $`Y`$ is also flag, as a consequence, rather than an imposed definition.

**Proof.** Necessity is Corollary 10.2, and sufficiency is Lemma 10.3. For a simplex of $`X`$, every pair of its vertices has cyclic distance one. The normal-crossing obstruction of Section 5 rules out an intersection between any component classes of those two vertices. Within each vertex the classes are already disjoint. The canonical representatives of their finite union therefore give one graph $`G`$. Lemma 10.3 places the simplex in $`Y`$. Finally a clique in the geometric graph makes every pair of component classes compatible by the already proved necessity. Canonical representatives realise the entire union simultaneously, and the sufficiency proves flagness. ∎
