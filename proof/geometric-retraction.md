# 11. Strong deformation retraction for Seifert-fibred exteriors

[Overview](../README.md) · [Geometric realisation](geometric-realisation.md) · [Decoder](matching-carrier.md)

**Theorem 11.2.** For every nonempty oriented non-split Seifert-fibred link exterior, the identity on vertices gives $`X\subseteq Y`$, and $`|X|`$ is a strong deformation retract of $`|Y|`$. Thus $`Y`$ is contractible. The point branches use the identity. In the matching branch the maps are the following formulas.

## 11.1. Construction and well-definedness of the retraction

If $`x\in |Y|`$ has barycentric coordinates

```math
x=\sum_{M\in\sigma}\lambda_M[M],\qquad
\lambda_M\ge0,\quad\sum_M\lambda_M=1,
```

choose the canonical graph $`G`$ of the component classes in $`\sigma`$ and set

```math
W(x)=\sum_{M\in\sigma}\lambda_M\chi_M\in P_G,
\qquad R(x)=S_G(W(x))\in |X_G|\subset |X|.
```

The notation $`W`$ records proper arc classes, not just endpoint pairings. We use its finite-coordinate interpretation on each simplex; we make no assertion identifying the global CW topology with a coordinate product or a metric topology.

**Lemma 11.1 (faces, support and choices).** The formula defines one map $`R:|Y|\to|X|`$. It is independent of the containing simplex, its zero barycentric coordinates, the support drawing, and the decoder's reference and root. Every matching occurring in $`R(x)`$ uses only edges of the positive support of $`W(x)`$.

**Proof.** All incidence vectors are nonnegative. Consequently $`W_e(x)=0`$ exactly when no positive-coefficient input matching uses $`e`$. On deleting zero coefficients, the new graph is precisely the old one with those zero-weight edges deleted, up to the canonical proper-arc identification. Theorem 6.1 says its decoder is the same on that face. The same argument compares the two graphs of any two containing simplices: both reduce to the graph of the positive vertex support of $`x`$. At a common face, extra edges outside that support may cross; they are first deleted and are never used to assert a common larger graph.

Canonical straightening, or Theorem 6.1's proper-arc isotopy invariance, removes the drawing choice. Theorem 6.1 removes the root/reference choices. Its edge-expectation identity and nonnegativity of output coefficients show that no positive output coefficient can use a zero-weight edge. Equivalently this follows directly from its zero-edge rounding rule. All choices therefore give the same point. ∎

On a closed simplex $`|\sigma|`$ the affine map $`W`$ lands in a fixed finite polytope $`P_G`$, and $`S_G`$ is continuous with image in the finite subcomplex $`X_G`$. Thus $`R|_{|\sigma|}`$ is continuous. Lemma 11.1 supplies agreement on faces. The weak topology of the simplicial realisation proves global continuity of $`R`$ into the CW realisation of $`X`$.

For $`x\in|X|`$, use its own simplex of $`X`$ in the formula. Exact recovery in Theorem 6.1 gives

```math
R(x)=x.
```

This proves the retraction identity on every point of $`|X|`$, not only on its vertices.

## 11.2. The strong deformation and its topology

Identify $`|X|`$ with its simplicial-subcomplex image in $`|Y|`$. Define in finite barycentric coordinates

```math
H(x,t)=(1-t)x+tR(x),\qquad 0\le t\le1.
```

This is a point of $`|Y|`$, for the following reason. If $`x\in|\sigma|`$ and $`G`$ is its support graph, then every vertex of $`\sigma`$ and every vertex in $`R(x)`$ belongs to $`\mathcal M(G)`$. Lemma 10.3 makes this entire finite set one genuine geometric simplex $`\Delta_G`$. The segment stays in that simplex. We do not claim it stays in the original $`\sigma`$, or that its union of vertices is a cyclic simplex.

On each $`|\sigma|\times I`$, both $`x`$ and $`R(x)`$ have continuous coordinates in the fixed finite simplex $`\Delta_G`$. Hence the displayed formula is an ordinary continuous homotopy there. Its values agree on $`|\sigma\cap\sigma'|\times I`$ by Lemma 11.1. The product $`|Y|\times I`$, with its **ordinary product topology**, is generated by these closed-simplex cylinders: a CW complex is generated by its finite subcomplexes, and taking the product with the compact interval preserves this property. This is exactly the cellwise homotopy criterion proved in [Hatcher, Algebraic Topology](../sources/bibliography.md#hatcher-circle-map-method-and-cw-conclusion), printed p.523 (PDF p.532), also covered by Theorem A.6 on printed p.524. Thus $`H`$ is globally continuous. No local finiteness, countability or metric realisation is assumed.

The defining identities are

```math
H(x,0)=x,\qquad H(x,1)=R(x)\in|X|,
\qquad H(a,t)=a\quad(a\in|X|).
```

They prove the strong deformation retraction claimed in the main theorem. A simplicial subcomplex has its induced CW topology ([Hatcher, Algebraic Topology](../sources/bibliography.md#hatcher-circle-map-method-and-cw-conclusion), printed p.520), so the identification of $`|X|`$ with its image causes no additional topological issue.

There is a useful stronger identity. Theorem 6.1's edge expectation gives $`W(R(x))=W(x)`$, so throughout the same finite simplex

```math
W(H(x,t))=W(x),\qquad R(H(x,t))=R(x).
```

For a fixed flow $`w`$, let $`G_w`$ be its positive-support graph. Every point with flow $`w`$ is in the finite simplex on $`\mathcal M(G_w)`$: a positive vertex coefficient cannot contain a zero-flow edge. For $`p\in|X|`$, the edge-expectation and exact-recovery identities give $`R(x)=p`$ if and only if $`W(x)=W(p)`$. Hence each nonempty fibre of $`R`$, with $`w=W(p)`$, is the finite convex polytope

```math
\left\{(\lambda_M)_{M\in\mathcal M(G_w)}:\quad
\lambda_M\ge0,\quad \sum_M\lambda_M=1,\quad
\sum_M\lambda_M\chi_M=w\right\}.
```

The homotopy contracts that fibre linearly to its distinguished decoded point. This fibre statement is an additional consequence, not a substitute for the global continuity check.

Finally compose $`R`$ with a contraction of $`X`$ from Theorem 7.1, after the first half of $`H`$, to contract $`Y`$. The theorem covers all orientations and every classified Seifert exterior. It asserts neither a comparison theorem for arbitrary graph-manifold exteriors nor a general result for arbitrary non-split links.

## 11.3. Two boundaries and the double-cone test

When $`k\ne0`$ the vertex set is one point, as already noted. When $`k=0,n=2`$ every vertex is a single annulus. For two compatible, distinct seam classes, the two capped edges form a Jordan cycle. Their incidence difference has a face potential equal to one on one side and zero on the other. Theorem 5.3 gives distance one. Thus the geometric and cyclic graphs agree in this case; Proposition 10.4 gives $`Y=X`$ (and both are flag). The retraction is the identity on their realisations.

For zero or one cone this is a singleton. For two retained cones it is **not generally a singleton**: choose two disjoint seams between the actual circles, with one cone in each region between the seams. They are not ordinarily isotopic on the four-holed $`P`$, and Theorem 4.1 keeps their inverse-image annuli distinct under ordinary three-dimensional isotopy. They give at least two vertices. Their complex is nevertheless contractible by Theorem 7.1 and the equality just proved.

For the four-actual-boundary double-lens example of Section 8, write the four matching vertices as $`M_{00},M_{01},M_{10},M_{11}`$. All four belong to one geometric tetrahedron by Lemma 10.3, while $`X_G`$ is the square triangulated by its true diagonal $`M_{00}M_{11}`$. The comparison retracts this tetrahedron onto that triangulated square. In particular

```math
R\left(\frac{[M_{01}]+[M_{10}]}2\right)
=\frac{[M_{00}]+[M_{11}]}2.
```

These two midpoints have the same edge flow. The segment joining them stays in the geometric tetrahedron and fixes that flow. Therefore the already known distance-two pair is accommodated, rather than excluded or converted into a false cyclic edge.
