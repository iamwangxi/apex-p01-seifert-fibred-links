# 40. Relative heights are essential: another genuine three-block test

For comparison keep two central actual signs $`(+,-)`$ but take both positive Hopf leaves with signs $`(+,+)`$. The sign sums are $`(0,2,2)`$, so


```math
(k_0,k_1,k_2)=(-4,0,0),\qquad(q_1,q_2)=(2,2).
```


Each zero leaf is a pair of pants with one coherent demand-two port and two unit actual ends of the same sign. Its unmarked joint arc diagram is unique. Nevertheless the central horizontal cluster has two fixed outer ports and relative state $`\mathbb Z`$. Lemma 31.1 supplies no zero-leaf loop that kills it.

There are two zero regions, each with a one-face tree support. Polyhedron (36.1) is the line with coordinates $`(0,t)`$. Its integral states are different ordinary global vertices, (36.2) gives distance $`|n-m|`$, and (36.3) gives the ordinary unit-interval triangulation. Thus $`X`$ is an integer line although **both local zero arc complexes are points**. All global spanning surfaces in this example are connected, so $`Y=X`$.

This is a concrete reason that the many-block proof must retain the relative level directions. It is not valid to replace (36.1) by edge flows only, or to infer the result from separate block contractibility.

More generally splice $`r\ge2`$ positive Hopf leaves, each with two positive actual fibres, to a negative Hopf centre whose actual sign sum is $`2(2-r)`$. For $`r=2`$ use the two balanced actual fibres above; for $`r>2`$ one may take $`2(r-2)`$ negative actual fibres. Distinguished components are unknots, so the meridian–longitude replacements again leave the ambient manifold $`S^3`$. Directly,


```math
k_{\rm centre}=-4,\qquad k_{\rm leaf}=0,\qquad q_e=2.
```


Each zero leaf has one face and a unique diagram. The potential polyhedron is $`\mathbb R^{r-1}`$, and $`X=Y`$ is its common-threshold integer triangulation. In particular $`r=3`$ gives a genuine eight-component example with a triangulated plane. These examples test arbitrarily many relative directions; none arises from a product of nontrivial local arc complexes.
