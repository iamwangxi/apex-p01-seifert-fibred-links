# 29. Graph-manifold theorems and proof guide

[Overview](../README.md) · [Definitions](objects.md) · [Scope](scope.md) · [References](../sources/bibliography.md)

All objects, ordinary ambient isotopies, actual oriented meridians, whole open cyclic blocks and weak/CW realisations are those of Section 1. Write $`\Phi=\phi`$ and $`\mathcal T`$ for the genuine JSJ torus collection. An **actual boundary** is a torus of $`\partial E(L)`$, as distinct from a boundary created by cutting along $`\mathcal T`$. For each Seifert block choose an oriented regular fibre $`h_v`$ and put $`k_v=\Phi(h_v)`$. A block is zero if $`k_v=0`$ and horizontal otherwise.

**Theorem 29.1 (graph-manifold links whose pieces meet the link).** Let $`L\subset S^3`$ be nonempty, oriented and non-split. Suppose its exterior is a graph manifold and every Seifert JSJ block has at least one actual link boundary torus. Arbitrarily many blocks, arbitrary component orientations and retained exceptional fibres are allowed. Then the original geometric complex $`Y(L)=IS(L)`$ is contractible. The identity on vertices gives a simplicial inclusion $`X(L)\subseteq Y(L)`$, and $`|X(L)|`$ is a strong deformation retract of $`|Y(L)|`$ in the ordinary weak/CW topology. In particular $`X(L)`$ is contractible.

Every incompressible spanning vertex, in every genus, is norm-minimising. Its minimum JSJ trace is coherent of cardinality $`q_e=\mathrm{div}(\Phi|_{T_e})>0`$ on each edge incident to a horizontal block and empty between two zero blocks. Zero pieces are ordinary joint vertical-annulus systems with multiplicities; a horizontal block has one connected horizontal piece. If there are $`s\ge1`$ connected zero regions after contracting empty edges, the ordinary vertex description retains $`s-1`$ relative integer heights. Fixed supports triangulate potential polyhedra with those unbounded directions. Edge multiplicities alone do not describe the global vertices.

**Proof guide.** For one block use Theorems 1.2 and 11.2, including their elementary cases. For a nonempty JSJ collection, [Section 30](graph-structure.md) proves the all-genus classification and norm assertion. [Section 31](graph-ordinary-annuli.md) proves distinct-end ordinary detection, zero port winding and efficient comparison, including the all-zero branch. [Section 32](graph-horizontal-phases.md) identifies multiport cluster phases and the integer tree quotient. [Sections 33](graph-packet-comparison.md)–[35](graph-class-distance.md) prove packet range reduction, horizontal filling with inherited real lifts and the ordinary global class minimum. [Section 36](graph-potential-carrier.md) identifies the actual integral states and proves exact common-threshold decoding and geometric realisation of cyclic simplices. [Section 37](graph-geometric-retraction.md) gives the geometric carrier and strong deformation retraction. [Section 38](graph-contractibility.md) gives finite inclusion nullhomotopies and the CW/Whitehead conclusion. These arguments prove the theorem. ∎

**Theorem 29.2 (horizontal-leaf gluing).** Suppose an actual oriented non-split $`S^3`$ link exterior has a genuine Seifert star decomposition. Every leaf has actual boundary and nonzero fibre evaluation. The central Seifert block may have any planar base, retained cones, active-port demands and actual boundary count, including zero. If its fibre evaluation is zero, ordinary vertices and their class distances are the integer-demand ordinary multiarc model of Sections 31–36 on the regular central base. If its fibre evaluation is nonzero, the entire vertex space is a point. In either case $`X`$ is contractible and a strong deformation retract of $`Y`$, which is therefore contractible.

**Proof.** Section 30 proves the requisite central classification without using an actual central boundary: in the zero case the coherent leaf traces force vertical central pieces; in the nonzero case they force all central horizontal components to have the same sign. The resulting primitive global phase map gives the singleton case. In the zero case, every horizontal leaf has one external port, so its relative phase quotient is trivial. The central annulus detector, packet comparison, global distance, decoder, geometric carrier and finite-height contraction in Sections 31–38 apply unchanged. No central connectivity assumption is needed in the all-horizontal branch. ∎

Theorem 29.2 extends beyond Theorem 29.1 only in its permitted boundary-free central block. It does not remove the actual-boundary hypothesis for arbitrary graph manifolds. Neither theorem asserts general geometric flagness; the retraction is proved directly for actual simultaneous geometric simplices. The Seifert and stipulated two-block flagness results in Sections 10–11 and 23–28 remain valid special cases.

The six-component example is computed in [Section 39](graph-six-component-example.md): $`X=Y`$ is an infinite tree with an infinite-valence vertex. [Section 40](graph-relative-height-example.md) gives an integer line despite both local zero arc spaces being points. The boundary-free companion-block obstruction and the unhandled hyperbolic case are recorded in [Section 9](scope.md).
