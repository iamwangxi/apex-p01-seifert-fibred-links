# Part 11. The resonant Hopf star and its geometric tree

[Overview](../README.md) · [Next page](part11-2.md) · Part 11, page 1 of 2

This part concerns the actual six-component link of Part 10, Proposition 11.6. It does not claim contractibility for every graph link. All isotopies below are ordinary ambient isotopies, with actual boundary allowed to move on its labelled boundary tori. Surfaces may be disconnected; closed components are excluded. The auxiliary bundle used for normal forms is never used as the deck cover defining cyclic distance.

## 1. Result and conventions

Let $`K_0,K_1,K_2`$ be three positively oriented regular Hopf fibres, with pairwise linking number one. Inside each $`N(K_i)`$, replace $`K_i`$ by one positive and one negative parallel of slope
```math
\ell_i=\lambda_i-2\mu_i.
```
Write $`L_3`$ for this oriented six-component link. Its exterior has a central Hopf pants block $`C`$ and three pants-product leaves $`V_i`$. Each leaf has its two actual boundaries, labelled $`+`$ and $`-`$, and one port $`T_i`$.

The vertices of $`Y`$ are the ordinary classes of reduced incompressible spanning systems. A finite set is a simplex when its whole members have simultaneous disjoint representatives. The cyclic complex $`X`$ has the same vertices and is the flag complex on class-minimum cyclic distance one. We use the whole open-complement definition of width, with a single deck group for the original link class $`\Phi`$.

**Theorem 1 (the six-component resonant star).** The complete complexes for $`L_3`$ satisfy
```math
X(L_3)=Y(L_3).
```
They are a simplicial tree. Their root is the three-annulus spanning system. Ordinary vertices, all edges, the exact cyclic distance, a finite parent algorithm, and a strong deformation retraction to the root are described below. Every full complexity sublevel is a rooted subtree.

In particular, the unbounded genus $`3t-1,3t`$ systems of Part 10, Proposition 11.6 lie at cyclic distance exactly two from the root. They are not evidence of a nonzero homology class.

The proof has two separate ingredients. Ordinary phase integers are retained. A formal four-holed sphere then detects a repeated-axis barrier. This barrier confines every edge between nonminimal horizontal vertices to one Farey tree sector.

## 2. The actual blocks and their unique horizontal mode

In $`H_1(C)`$, the meridians form a basis and
```math
[\lambda_i]=\sum_{j\ne i}[\mu_j],\qquad
h_C=\mu_i+\lambda_i.
```
Opposite oriented winding in each pattern gives $`\Phi|_C=0`$. On $`V_i`$, $`\Phi`$ takes values $`+1,-1`$ on the actual local meridians and kills its Seifert fibre $`\ell_i`$. The two Seifert fibres on $`T_i`$ have intersection three. Thus all four blocks and the three interfaces are genuine. Incompressibility of the gluing tori and irreducibility of the pieces prove that the actual link is non-split.

The signed Seifert-piece classification of Part 7 applies to efficient cuts, including cancelling pieces. A leaf has nonzero restriction and zero fibre evaluation, so its pieces are vertical annuli. Their base arcs are essential. An inefficient boundary-parallel cut annulus is removed before reading the data.

If a central piece is horizontal, its positive primitive dual must kill every $`\ell_i`$. These equations are
```math
(J-3I)(\eta(\mu_0),\eta(\mu_1),\eta(\mu_2))^t=0.
```
The kernel is precisely the line generated by $`(1,1,1)`$. Choose its positive primitive generator $`\eta`$. Then
```math
\eta(\mu_i)=1,\quad \eta(\lambda_i)=2,\quad \eta(h_C)=3.
```
Its connected fibre $`R`$ has
```math
\chi(R)=-3,\qquad |\partial R\cap T_i|=1,\qquad g(R)=1.
```
The port degree is one because $`\gcd(1,2)=1`$. The local horizontal classification and positive interpolation imply that every finite central horizontal collection consists of parallel copies of this one fibre, with either orientation. For clarity, one can obtain the bundle directly: a primitive class positive on $`h_C`$ has a map transverse to the Seifert fibres; its level surface covers the pants three times and cutting along it gives an interval product.

A central vertical trace would have slope $`h_C`$, different from every leaf fibre. It cannot match a leaf. Hence a nonhorizontal central system is empty, since a remaining closed component is forbidden. This leaves exactly two modes: empty centre or parallel horizontal fibres of $`R`$. There is no additional mixed central mode.

## 3. Complete signed cut diagrams

### 3.1. The annular vertex

An empty centre leaves one actual-to-actual seam in each leaf pants. Each seam has one ordinary class with free endpoints. Their vertical annuli give one vertex, denoted $`A`$. There is no phase integer when all ports are unused.

### 3.2. Horizontal diagrams and the pants reflection

Suppose the centre is nonempty. Since its total class is zero, it consists of $`2r`$ copies of $`R`$, $`r`$ positive and $`r`$ negative, with $`r\ge1`$. Give these fibres their common positive cyclic order
```math
j\in\mathbb Z/(2r).
```
At each leaf both actual boundaries are tethered to the port. Indeed, an actual-to-actual seam separates the two actual holes from every essential port-to-port return; it cannot coexist with such a return. If it used the actual boundaries, all nonempty port trace would have to be paired by returns, giving this contradiction.

The other $`2r-2`$ ends form $`r-1`$ parallel essential returns. A returning arc separates the two actual holes. The positive actual tether occupies position $`a_i`$, the negative one position $`a_i+r`$, and the return matching is
```math
P_{a_i}(j)=2a_i-j\pmod {2r}\quad(j\ne a_i,a_i+r).
\qquad\text{(3.1)}
```
This follows also by cutting the pants along the two tethers: the return endpoints appear in two intervals, with reverse matching. Conversely this matching, with the two fixed positions as actual tethers, is embedded by the unique essential wave packet in the pants.

If $`\epsilon_j`$ is the sign of the $`j`$-th central fibre, compatibility says
```math
\epsilon_{a_i}=+1,\quad \epsilon_{a_i+r}=-1,\quad
\epsilon_{2a_i-j}=-\epsilon_j
\qquad\text{(3.2)}
```
away from the fixed positions. The returns have no actual ends. In fact these $`r-1`$ returns and the two tethers cut a leaf pants into $`r`$ disk regions. We do not assume that its complement is connected.

### 3.3. Why only two axes occur

Here is a self-contained form of the diagram calculation of Part 10, §11. For $`z=\exp(\pi\sqrt{-1}/r)`$ and $`E(z)=\sum\epsilon_jz^j`$, an axis satisfying (3.2) gives
```math
E(z)+z^{2a}\overline{E(z)}=4z^a,\qquad
\mathrm{Re}(z^{-a}E(z))=2.
```
The corresponding $`z^a`$ lie on the intersection of a line and the unit circle. There are at most two.

Use the positive actual tether at leaf zero to label $`a_0=0`$. For $`r=1`$ the only positive axis is zero, and all three leaves use it. For $`r>1`$ all three axes cannot agree: each nonfixed pair would then assemble into a whole closed component. Thus exactly two axes occur, $`0,d`$. Reducedness and the signs give precisely
```math
1\le d\lt 2r,\qquad \gcd(d,r)=1,\qquad d+r\ \hbox{odd}.
\qquad\text{(3.3)}
```
To verify necessity and sufficiency, the two reflections generate translation by $`2d`$. If $`\gcd(d,r)>1`$, a reflection orbit misses all fixed actual ends and is closed. If the gcd is one, the orbits are the even and odd positions, each a path with $`r`$ vertices. Propagating opposite signs along its $`r-1`$ edges is consistent with the actual signs exactly when $`d+r`$ is odd. The prescribed actual defects seed a unique sign word, including the negative seed on the odd orbit when $`r`$ is odd. Repeating either reflection at the third leaf changes no connectivity.

Let
```math
\varnothing\ne B\subseteq\{1,2\},\qquad
a_i=d\ (i\in B),\quad a_i=0\ (i\notin B).
\qquad\text{(3.4)}
```
Thus there are three choices of the repeated-axis partition. Every (3.3)–(3.4) diagram is reduced and has exactly two whole components. Its cuts are essential, so the standard innermost-disk assembly proves incompressibility. The corresponding outermost-arc argument gives boundary-incompressibility at the actual tori, each of which carries just one boundary curve. The global complement is connected: meridians at the actual boundaries detect the individual components independently. This also follows from the usual reduced spanning-system argument.

For odd $`r`$, both components have three actual boundaries and genus $`(3r-1)/2`$. For even $`r`$, their boundary counts are four and two and their genera are $`3r/2-1,3r/2`$. In either case
```math
c(S)=\chi_-(S)=6r.
\qquad\text{(3.5)}
```
More precisely, for odd $`r`$ the even-position component contains all three positive actual boundaries, and the odd-position component all three negative ones. For even $`r`$, set $`A_0=\{0,1,2\}\setminus B`$. The even-position component contains both actual boundaries at the leaves in $`A_0`$, and the odd-position component both at the leaves in $`B`$. Their genera are respectively $`1-|A_0|+3r/2`$ and $`1-|B|+3r/2`$. In this even case each whole component has zero central dual class although it contains $`r`$ essential horizontal fibres of mixed signs.

## 4. The full ordinary quotient: two integers remain

This section is essential: counting the diagrams in §3 alone does not classify vertices.

Fix a positive bundle map $`p_0:C\to\mathbb R/\mathbb Z`$ in class $`\eta`$, with linear port restrictions. The primitive quotient coordinate on $`T_i/\ell_i`$ is denoted $`\theta_i`$. Put each leaf in its standard wave-packet form: positive tether at $`\theta_i=0`$, negative tether at $`1/2`$, and equally spaced port points. With levels $`j/(2r)`$, a central map $`p=p_0+u`$ has boundary phase constants
```math
\alpha_i=\frac{a_i}{2r}+n_i,\qquad n_i\in\mathbb Z.
\qquad\text{(4.1)}
```
Changing the real lift $`u`$ adds one common integer. Normalise $`n_0=0`$, and write
```math
z_1=n_1-n_0,\quad z_2=n_2-n_0,\quad
L_0=0,\quad L_i=a_i+2r z_i.
\qquad\text{(4.2)}
```
Here $`L_i`$ is an integer lift of the tether axis; it is not a deck index for $`\Phi`$.

**Lemma 4 (ordinary catalogue).** Besides $`A`$, every ordinary vertex is uniquely one of
```math
\begin{array}{ll}
H(1;z_1,z_2),& (z_1,z_2)\in\mathbb Z^2;\\ 
H(r,B,d;z_1,z_2),&
r>1,\ B\text{ as in (3.4)},\ d\text{ as in (3.3)},\ 
(z_1,z_2)\in\mathbb Z^2 .
\end{array}
\qquad\text{(4.3)}
```
There is no extra endcap bit, independent return twist, cyclic relabelling, or boundary-motion quotient.

**Proof.** First localise an ordinary isotopy at the JSJ tori. Complexity distinguishes $`A`$ and determines $`r`$ among horizontal representatives, whose efficient interface slopes are all $`\ell_i`$. Put the two systems and their actual boundaries in general position, with parallel torus traces disjoint, and use the relative-product comparison underlying the repaired Lemma 31.2.

A whole JSJ torus inside a relative product would inject $`\mathbb Z^2`$ through a free surface group. A returning annulus of its intersection with the torus collection has both boundaries on one product face. Retraction to that face shows that the two curves cobound a face annulus. The union torus bounds its annular parallelism solid torus: a higher longitudinal degree would make an essential simple face curve a proper power in the free surface group. Choose an innermost such parallelism. A spanning torus-annulus cannot enter this solid torus and reach the other face without crossing its boundary annulus; nested returning annuli are removed innermost first. Thus the innermost face annulus has no other torus trace. It is a complete cut piece parallel to the same JSJ torus, contrary to efficiency.

All remaining torus-annuli span the product. Cutting the face into disks straightens them simultaneously to interval products, so the product push preserves the original torus collection. Repeating the product comparison gives a torus-preserving ordinary isotopy. A global spanning-bundle exception is impossible because $`\Phi`$ kills the injected central torus group. Nor is an individual whole component a global fibre: each misses an actual boundary torus, whose injected rank-two group is killed by that component's dual class.

This argument uses the product comparison itself. It does not use the old assertion that all zero-evaluation blocks have empty trace; that assertion fails in this example.

Consequently a global ordinary comparison restricts to the joint pants diagrams and central positive-fibre data. The unique positive actual tether at leaf zero fixes label zero; the positive cyclic order fixes every other label. There is no reversal: an isotopy induces the identity on the primitive class $`\eta`$.

There is also no port winding hidden in an unmarked leaf loop. Track its positive actual-to-port tether. In the free pants group the endpoint equation is
```math
d_+^{\,m}w d_P^{-k}=w.
```
The independent peripheral homology classes of $`d_+`$ and $`d_P`$ force $`m=k=0`$. A finite permutation of the parallel returns does not change the conclusion; alternatively pass to a finite power and divide its winding. This proof uses an actual tether to control the entire packet, but does not require any actual end on a return.

For the central part, all positive maps in class $`\eta`$ differ from $`p_0`$ by a real function, unique up to a global integer. Positivity along $`h_C`$ is a convex condition on that function. Fixed ordered port traces can first be straightened to primitive linear quotient coordinates; their integer boundary differences are (4.1). With the same boundary differences, positive interpolation identifies the level systems by an isotopy relative to those traces. Thus the relative components are exactly
```math
H^0(\partial C;\mathbb Z)/H^0(C;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z^3/\mathbb Z=\mathbb Z^2.
```
The fact that $`\eta(h_C)=3`$ does not replace this by a quotient modulo three: $`\eta`$ is primitive and its port degree is one. Conversely choose a real function on the central Seifert base with the prescribed constants (4.1) at the three boundaries. Adding it to $`p_0`$ preserves its positive derivative along $`h_C`$, so every integer pair is realised.

To check injectivity under an isotopy that need not stay horizontal, track the positively oriented central fibre $`R_0`$, fixed by the actual tether at leaf zero. Once the axes have been identified, standard representatives with phases $`z,z'`$ have the same boundary circles for this fibre. Their difference is an absolute two-cycle in $`C`$. Interpolating the positive maps from one phase vector to the other sweeps the $`i`$-th boundary circle through $`z_i'-z_i`$ full transverse turns. Orient the boundary tori accordingly. The trace-chain identity gives
```math
[R_0(z')-R_0(z)]=
\sum_{i=0}^{2}(n_i'-n_i)[T_i]\quad\hbox{in }H_2(C;\mathbb Z).
\qquad\text{(4.4)}
```
The boundary torus classes have exactly one relation, $`[T_0]+[T_1]+[T_2]=0`$: this is the kernel of $`H_2(\partial C)\to H_2(C)`$, by the relative fundamental-class sequence. An arbitrary torus-preserving ordinary isotopy instead has zero normal boundary winding, by the tether-loop calculation. Its trace-chain identity makes the left side of (4.4) zero. Thus its phase changes are diagonal, and $`n_0=0`$ gives $`z'=z`$. This detects the integers under arbitrary ordinary isotopy, not only positive-map interpolation.

Tangential motion along $`\ell_i`$ adds no parameter to a vertical annulus, which already contains the whole fibre circle; it also sweeps no torus class in (4.4). The complexity (3.5) distinguishes the empty mode and different $`r`$, and the tracked joint diagrams distinguish the axes and signs. Positive interpolation with the same integers proves there are no further states. This proves both surjectivity and injectivity of (4.3). ∎

## 5. A formal four-holed sphere, with its winding retained

For two distinct leaves $`e,f`$, join their port circles by a formal annulus and call the resulting four-holed sphere $`Q_{ef}`$. Its four labelled holes are the two actual holes of each pants. This is a two-dimensional encoding of positive phase data, not an assertion that an annulus between the slopes $`\ell_e,\ell_f`$ embeds in $`C`$.

Use the global real difference $`u=p-p_0`$ in this connector. Interpolate its two boundary lifts in an annular map of degree one, positive in the circle direction. Its level arcs join the central labels. The projected diagram $`\Pi_{ef}(S)`$ comprises the two leaf wave packets and these connectors. Its normal parameters are
```math
2r\ \hbox{crossings of the joining circle},\qquad
D_{ef}=L_f-L_e.
\qquad\text{(5.1)}
```
A full relative phase turn changes $`D_{ef}`$ by $`2r`$. Omitting that turn would erase an ordinary torus twist.

Three facts about this encoding will be used.

1. It is invariant under the ordinary comparison of §4. The leaf reference diagrams fix the marking, and the connector uses the real boundary differences, rather than just their residues.
2. A disjoint whole family in the three-manifold projects to a disjoint family on every $`Q_{ef}`$. For simultaneous efficiency, treat the labelled union as one incompressible surface collection, minimise its torus intersections, and remove a boundary parallelism outermost in the whole collection. These ambient packet pushes keep the members disjoint. Local classification and joint straightening then apply to the union, without requiring its cut complements to be connected. Its central horizontal fibres are levels of one positive map; all selected level subsets use the same connector. For an individual member, an increasing degree-one reparametrisation sends its selected levels to $`j/(2r)`$. Such a lift satisfies $`\rho(t+m)=\rho(t)+m`$, so it preserves the retained full-turn boundary integers and gives exactly the member's ordinary diagram, not a new phase choice. Positive interpolation between the two connector boundary maps preserves their order. Joint leaf straightening supplies the disjoint wave packets.
3. Conversely, disjoint matching diagrams in one $`Q_{ef}`$, together with a duplicate of one leaf packet and the prescribed within-duplicate phase, lift to disjoint whole systems in $`C\cup V_0\cup V_1\cup V_2`$. Choose the common cyclic level order from the diagrams, duplicate the required packet, and prescribe the three increasing port circle maps. Their real differences from $`p_0`$ extend over $`C`$ while preserving positivity. Explicitly, for boundary increasing lifts $`\theta+u_i(\theta)`$, use nonnegative base cutoff functions $`\rho_i`$ with disjoint collars and set
```math
p=p_0+\sum_i\rho_i u_i(p_0).
```
Its fibre derivative is a positive multiple of $`1+\sum_i\rho_i u_i'`$, a convex combination of positive derivatives. Constants in the $`u_i`$ retain the desired turns. Its selected levels and the leaf arcs give whole disjoint representatives. Their axes, free four-hole slopes and within-duplicate integers are the prescribed ones, so the ordinary catalogue of §4 identifies them with the given input vertices. All their pieces are essential and the reduced reflection graphs reach actual boundary; essential assembly introduces no compression or closed-component deletion.

No component of a projected diagram has a bigon with the joining circle: a bigon would be a boundary-parallel port return in one pants. Thus its crossing count is minimal.

### 5.1. The elementary four-hole calculation

Cap the four holes to marked points and take the double branched torus $`\mathbb R^2/\mathbb Z^2`$, with involution $`(x,y)\mapsto(-x,-y)`$. Put $`e+,e-,f+,f-`$ at $`(0,0),(1/2,0),(0,1/2),(1/2,1/2)`$. The joining circle lifts to $`y=1/4,3/4`$.

A primitive matching lifts to two parallel essential primitive torus curves. Conversely the two involution-invariant parallel curves in a primitive direction give its matching. There is no extra isotopy state here: cut the torus at the two curves, straighten one of the complementary annuli, and extend the straightening over the other by the involution. The four branch points on the boundary curves retain their labels. Thus the primitive direction, up to simultaneous sign, classifies the matching.

Use the direction $`(-p,r)`$, with $`r>0`$, as the sign convention for slope $`p/r`$. The matching has $`2r`$ crossings of the joining circle. These crossings have spacing $`1/(2r)`$ in the linear model. The two positive tethers meet the representative $`y=1/4`$ at
```math
x=-p/(4r),\qquad x=p/(4r)
```
respectively. Their cyclic label offset is therefore $`p\pmod {2r}`$. Comparing with (5.1) gives
```math
p\equiv D_{ef}\pmod {2r}.
```
A full connector turn is a Dehn twist at the joining circle, changing $`p`$ by $`2r`$; its retained real lift fixes $`p=D_{ef}`$, not merely the congruence class. Cutting the linear model also gives exactly the reflections $`j\mapsto-j`$ and $`j\mapsto2p-j`$.

The three parity classes of primitive $`(p,r)`$ give the three pairings of four points: even $`p`$, odd $`r`$ pairs like actual signs across the two pants; odd $`p`$, even $`r`$ pairs the two actual holes within each pants; odd $`p,r`$ gives the remaining cross-pairing. The orientations on one of the two pants are reversed for the formal gluing. The first two pairings join opposite virtual signs, while the third joins equal signs. The allowed classes are exactly
```math
\gcd(p,r)=1,\qquad p+r\ \hbox{odd}.
\qquad\text{(5.2)}
```
Two distinct primitive allowed matchings are disjoint exactly when
```math
|ps-qr|=1.
\qquad\text{(5.3)}
```
For completeness, disjoint distinct matchings cannot have the same pairing: two different arcs joining the same pair would cut off an empty bigon, since the other matched pair must remain on the same side. With different pairings their four arcs form a quadrilateral on the marked sphere. Each matching lifts to two parallel torus curves. The two lifted pairs meet at exactly the four branch points and nowhere else; hence the primitive slope determinant is one. Conversely two primitive torus directions of determinant one have representatives through the branch points whose only four intersections are those points. Quotienting gives the disjoint matchings. This proves (5.3), including necessity, rather than identifying geometric edges with intersection of arbitrary closed curves.

There is a second, nonprimitive possibility important here. If the two leaves have the same axis, put
```math
k=(L_f-L_e)/(2r)\in\mathbb Z.
```
The diagram consists of the two arcs of the slope $`2k/1`$ matching and $`r-1`$ parallel essential closed curves of that same slope. This is immediate from gluing the equal reflection pairs; equivalently it is the normal multicurve of parameters $`(r,2rk)`$. The closed curves separate the two arc neighbourhoods. They are auxiliary diagram curves, not forbidden closed components of the original spanning system.

**Repeated-axis barrier.** If $`r>1`$ and a disjoint whole system $`T`$ is projected to this $`Q_{ef}`$, its diagram must have the same axis at $`e,f`$ and the same integer $`k`$.

Indeed it misses an essential closed curve of slope $`2k/1`$. Cutting along that curve gives two pants. In either pants the two actual endpoints have a unique joining seam. Thus the projected diagram is those same two arcs with parallel copies of the separating curve, and no different primitive slope is possible. Its normal parameters are $`(s,2sk)`$, proving the assertion. This is the extra constraint supplied by an actual-free return packet.

## 6. Exact geometric edge criterion

For a horizontal vertex let $`r`$ and $`L=(0,L_1,L_2)`$ be its data. For another use $`s`$ and $`M=(0,M_1,M_2)`$.

**Theorem 6 (all edges).**

- $`A`$ is adjacent exactly to the vertices with $`r=1`$.
- Two distinct horizontal vertices are adjacent exactly when
```math
\boxed{\quad |sL_i-rM_i|\le1\quad(i=1,2).\quad}
\qquad\text{(6.1)}
```

Here are equivalent sector coordinates, which explain the criterion. For $`r>1`$ there is a unique pair of repeated-axis leaves. Its relative phase is fixed along every edge to another $`s>1`$ vertex, by the repeated-axis barrier. The other projected four-hole matching then has primitive slope $`p/r`$ satisfying (5.2).

There are three types of sector:

| repeated leaves | fixed phase | free slope |
|---|---|---|
| $`0,1`$ | $`z_1=k`$ | $`p/r=(d+2rz_2)/r`$, with $`B=\{2\}`$ |
| $`0,2`$ | $`z_2=k`$ | $`p/r=(d+2rz_1)/r`$, with $`B=\{1\}`$ |
| $`1,2`$ | $`z_2-z_1=k`$ | $`p/r=(d+2rz_1)/r`$, with $`B=\{1,2\}`$ |

A sector also contains $`A=1/0`$ and its $`r=1`$ vertices, whose free slope is $`2m/1`$. In that sector edges are exactly (5.3). Distinct sectors intersect only at $`A`$, or at one edge $`A\,H(1;z_1,z_2)`$.

**Proof.** The annular assertion follows from the pants seam: it crosses every essential return, while for $`r=1`$ it misses the two tethers in every leaf. Phase changes can be supported away from the annular seam, so no integer is excluded.

Let $`r>1`$ and let a horizontal neighbour be given. Its projection at the repeated pair must preserve that pair and its integer $`k`$. If its own sheet number is greater than one, its two distinct axes must consequently give the same repeated pair. Its remaining matching is primitive and oriented, so (5.3) is necessary. The converse is the lifting construction in §5, duplicating the common repeated packet. This also covers a neighbour with sheet number one. Distinct $`r=s=1`$ vertices are not adjacent: their projections are primitive even-integer slopes, which are either equal or have determinant at least two; equality in both projections gives the same vertex.

For example, in the first sector the repeated-pair condition is $`z_1=w_1`$, and the determinant condition is
```math
|s(d+2rz_2)-r(e+2sw_2)|=1.
```
These are exactly (6.1). To see the reverse arithmetic implication across possible sectors, different repeated pairs force some determinant to have absolute value at least $`\min(r,s)>1`$. Within one pair a nonzero difference in $`k`$ contributes $`2rs`$; the remaining coordinate cannot reduce both determinants to absolute value at most one. With a sheet-number-one endpoint the same calculation fixes its within-pair phase and gives the determinant-one free coordinate. This proves (6.1) in every case. ∎

An explicit common central order for an edge of free slopes $`p/r,q/s`$ is obtained by merging, modulo one, the two labelled point sets
```math
\{-p/(4r)+j/(2r):0\le j\lt 2r\},\qquad
\{-q/(4s)+k/(2s):0\le k\lt 2s\}.
\qquad\text{(6.2)}
```
They have no coincidences when $`|ps-qr|=1`$. Rotate the merged order to start with the two positive tethers. The pillowcase construction gives the return matching at the free ports; the other leaf is duplicated with its fixed integer. Thus (6.2), followed by the positive extension in §5, constructs actual disjoint representatives without an unbounded search.

## 7. The global tree and the parent algorithm

The allowed Farey graph in a sector is a tree, not the full Farey graph. Here is a proof that also supplies its parent.

For a reduced $`p/r`$ with $`r>1`$ and $`p+r`$ odd, exactly one reduced $`q/s`$ satisfies
```math
0\lt s\lt r,\qquad |ps-qr|=1,\qquad q+s\ \hbox{odd}.
\qquad\text{(7.1)}
```
There are the usual two determinant-one candidates with denominators $`s`$ and $`r-s`$; precisely one has the allowed parity. More explicitly, if $`u`$ is the inverse of $`p`$ modulo $`r`$, test
```math
(q,s)=((pu-1)/r,u),\qquad
((p(r-u)+1)/r,r-u).
\qquad\text{(7.2)}
```
Exactly one numerator plus denominator is odd. This remains valid for negative $`p`$. Distinct allowed neighbours cannot have the same positive denominator.

Define the parent of a vertex with $`r>1`$ by (7.1) in its unique sector, retaining its fixed phase. If $`s=1`$, $`q`$ is even and gives the appropriate $`H(1;z_1,z_2)`$. Otherwise write
```math
d'=q\bmod 2s,\qquad m'=(q-d')/(2s)
```
and use the sector table to recover its data. Every $`r=1`$ vertex has parent $`A`$.

This reaches $`A`$ in at most $`r`$ steps. Every edge has its higher-$`r`$ endpoint as a child of its lower-$`r`$ endpoint. This includes the root edges. Thus all vertices are connected to $`A`$, and a cycle would have a maximum-$`r`$ vertex with two different lower parents, impossible. No triangle exists, so a geometric simplex has at most two distinct vertices. The entire geometric complex is this tree.

In particular, it was unnecessary to assume that the initial geometric complex was flag, or to replace it by a flag completion. Its actual whole-system edge classification proves that it has no higher simplex.

## 8. The original cyclic width and exact class distances

We first spell out the whole-complement range rule being used. Every spanning system here has connected whole complement, and $`\Phi`$ vanishes on loops in that complement, since those loops have intersection zero with the system. A component of its inverse image in the original cyclic cover therefore projects homeomorphically to the whole open complement. For two representatives $`S,T`$, the current $`S-T`$ has zero periods and defines an integral chamber potential $`H`$, unique up to one global constant. Within one whole $`T`$-block, its values, up to sign and one constant, are exactly the indices of the whole $`S`$-blocks met there. Every downstairs chamber occurs in that block. Consequently
```math
D(S,T)=\max H-\min H.
\qquad\text{(8.0)}
```
This reads all local chambers in one comparison; it neither chooses a lift independently in a leaf nor identifies the auxiliary $`\eta`$-phase with the $`\Phi`$-deck coordinate.

First check an edge of $`Y`$. In a nonannular sector it is represented by two different primitive matchings of the formal four-hole sphere. Their pairings differ; joining the two occurrences at each actual boundary makes their oriented difference one embedded closed quadrilateral curve. Its chamber potential has only two consecutive values. Restricting to the joining circle shows that the signed central gap walk has oscillation one.

Now return to the three-manifold. Give the central gaps the values of that walk. The original current $`S-T`$ has zero periods, since both systems represent $`\Phi`$, so these values extend to one global integral potential. Every chamber of a leaf cut by the union of its tethers and returns reaches its port. Hence no new value is introduced in a leaf, even though there are many disconnected leaf chambers. Both central values occur in nonempty open chambers. The whole-complement representative width is therefore one.

For $`A`$ and an $`r=1`$ vertex the same conclusion follows directly from the two oppositely signed central fibres and the three unused actual-to-actual seams. There are again exactly two potential values. Distinct ordinary vertices have class distance at least one. Thus every geometric edge is a cyclic edge.

Conversely Part 7, Theorem D, or its minimum-position edge argument alone, gives $`X\subseteq Y`$: a transverse intersection has four local difference values $`h,h+1,h,h-1`$, incompatible with width one, and coincident components can be separated. Consequently the edge sets agree. Their graph is a tree, so flag completion adds nothing:
```math
X=Y.
\qquad\text{(8.1)}
```
Thus the requested strong deformation retraction of $`|Y|`$ onto $`|X|`$ is the identity in this family; §9 gives the further nontrivial contraction to $`A`$.

Banks Proposition 2.4, with the explicit all-incompressible extension after Definition 10.1, identifies the cyclic class minimum with the graph metric for this cyclic convention. If $`h(S)`$ is its number of parent steps and $`W`$ is the first common vertex on the two parent paths, then
```math
\boxed{\delta(S,T)=h(S)+h(T)-2h(W).}
\qquad\text{(8.2)}
```
This is a terminating criterion on the complete ordinary data. There are no independently adjustable leaf deck displacements.

There is also a direct class-minimum formula for distance from the root. Put $`u_0=0`$ and $`u_{j+1}=u_j-\epsilon_j`$. Then
```math
\boxed{\delta(A,S)=\max_j u_j-\min_j u_j=h(S).}
\qquad\text{(8.3)}
```
Here is a minimum-position verification independent of (8.2). Choose the annular seam to cross each essential pants return once and miss the tethers. Every intersection circle is essential. Cutting $`S`$ at these circles leaves pieces containing at least one whole $`R`$; their Euler characteristics are strictly negative. Thus no two intersection circles cobound an annulus in $`S`$, and no one is parallel to an actual boundary through an annulus. A product region would have an annular face on $`A`$ and such an annular face on $`S`$, or an inessential disk face. Neither is possible. The pair has simplified intersection and attains the class minimum by the printed all-incompressible minimum-position input.

In the central block the single global difference potential has precisely the successive values $`u_j`$. Cutting a leaf pants at the annular seam produces an annulus; tethers and the halves of returns are spanning arcs, so each resulting chamber meets the port. No additional extremum occurs there. Thus its whole-complement width is exactly the oscillation in (8.3). This proof allows disconnected local complements and actual-free return annuli.

For the family displayed in Part 10, Proposition 11.6, $`r=2t,d=1`$, and the fixed phase is zero. Its free slope $`1/(2t)`$ has parent $`0/1`$. Hence its height and root distance are two, although both component genera are unbounded.

For example, in any zero-fixed-phase sector, the free slopes $`0/1,1/2,2/3,3/4`$ have successive heights $`1,2,3,4`$; the slope $`1/4`$ instead has height two. Distinct $`r=1`$ phase vertices have distance two through $`A`$. These distinguish genus growth from cyclic depth.

## 9. Retraction, exchange, and complexity sublevels

Give each edge length one. Move $`x`$ along the unique finite path from $`A`$ to $`x`$, leaving distance $`(1-t)d(A,x)`$ from $`A`$. This fixes $`A`$ and defines a strong deformation retraction to it. On every closed edge cylinder its image lies in the finite union of the two endpoint parent paths. The CW cylinder criterion, with compact interval factor, proves continuity in the weak realisation topology; local finiteness is unnecessary.

Every parent has strictly smaller $`c=\chi_-`$, until $`A`$, and so every full complexity sublevel is a rooted subtree and the same contraction preserves it. For this link the maximal component genus is also strictly increasing with $`r`$, giving the analogous statement for that genus bound. These sublevels still have infinitely many phase vertices; a finite phase box is an additional numerical truncation.

The competition paper's full exchange axiom now holds as a consequence, without choosing compatible surgeries. Vertices at distance two in a tree have exactly one common neighbour. It is an apex of their entire common neighbourhood. It has lower $`r`$, and hence lower complexity, than at least one endpoint, because a vertex cannot have two distinct lower parents. This proves both exchange conditions literally. The direct tree contraction already proves contractibility, so this invocation is not needed to infer a geometric axiom from a finite calculation.
