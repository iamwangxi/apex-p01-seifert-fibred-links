# 9. Scope, dependencies and verification boundary

[Overview](../README.md) · [Definitions](objects.md) · [References](../sources/bibliography.md)

Theorem 1.2 concerns the reduced **cyclic-distance flag complex** of all incompressible spanning surfaces for nonempty oriented non-split links in $`S^3`$ with Seifert-fibred exterior. The proof includes solid-torus exteriors, either sign of each circle-action weight, arbitrary component orientations, the keychain alternative, zero/one/two retained cones of every allowed order, and every even actual boundary count in the balanced branch. A chosen fibering with $`k\ne0`$ has one vertex; the $`k=0`$ branch has the marked planar matching model. Sections 10–11 extend this to the original geometric complex. Sections 12–28 additionally handle the specified two-block splice family, without asserting a theorem for general graph-manifold or other JSJ exteriors.

The competition paper's Theorem A handles knots; Theorem D handles linking-indecomposable links, with the condition forcing spanning surfaces to be connected. Its Introduction, p. 2, lists general links among the untreated extensions and identifies the shared-component obstruction at Lemma 4.9. Our object permits disconnected surfaces and uses the class minimum of complete open cyclic blocks. For connected spanning surfaces, distance-one adjacency agrees with disjointness: a connected disjoint representative lies in one complete cut block and yields width at most one, while the simplified-position width-one argument gives almost-disjoint representatives, which for distinct connected classes are disjoint. The ordinary simultaneous-position theorem gives flagness of the geometric complex in this range; see Sakuma Proposition 4.9(1), p. 883, and Kakimizu's conventions. Thus the complexes agree on the common connected-surface range. Equality of the complexes is not asserted for arbitrary disconnected surfaces: Sections 10–11 instead prove a strong deformation retraction of the geometric complex onto the cyclic one. The example in §8 explains why that distinction matters.

## Dependency ledger

- Hatcher's Lemmas 1.10–1.11, Proposition 1.12, the horizontal product argument, Proposition 1.13, Corollary 1.15, section/filling models and genus-one gluing support §2 and simultaneous verticalisation in §4. The actual filling classification is derived in §2.2, including the fibre-meridian alternative; it is not attributed to an unstated classification theorem.
- Schultens' Proposition 25 is used **only for closed good aspherical orbifolds**, after the double, genuine surface cover, infinite-order essentiality and group retraction have been proved in §3. Its Remark 35 is not used as a boundary-arc theorem. The homotopy-to-isotopy passage back to the punctured ordinary surface uses standard ordinary surface topology.

Its proof uses the possibly noncompact Proposition 3.3, presented there as a version of Sakuma 1994 Proposition 4.8(2), via Corollary 3.4. We retain this as an explicit literature input. We have not independently reconstructed that noncompact theorem from Sakuma's proof.
- No projection, order, dismantling or contractibility result from PS §§4–7 is used. Proposition 2.3, whose reverse inequality is deferred to §4, is not used either. The proof makes no wider assessment of PS's results.
- Kakimizu 1992 Theorem A supplies **geometric connectivity** only in the all-incompressible fibred point argument. No disconnected-surface identification of cyclic distance with a geometric path metric is needed.
- Ordinary curve/arc homotopy-isotopy, half-bigons, isotopy extension and canonical multiarcs are the standard surface facts recorded by Farb–Margalit and Epstein. Hatcher's Proposition A.1 and Whitehead Theorem 4.5 supply the final CW step.
- Hatcher's 2008 circle-map proof is the method source for the finite band-height family. Planar circulation and matching-height methods belong to the classical framework exemplified by Propp. All fixed-graph algebra and rounding assertions used here are proved in §6, including disconnected graphs, bridges and non-disk regions. No printed connected/forced-edge hypothesis is extended by citation.

## What the checks establish

The underlying proof received two separate adversarial reviews in fresh contexts: topology/classification, and carrier/contractibility. Neither reported a fatal or repairable mathematical issue; one wording correction explicitly identifies the fibre quotient of the fundamental groupoid in §4.2. A separate fresh-context package review checked proof coverage, source alignment, references and reproducibility; it did not repeat the mathematical reviews.

The Seifert cyclic proof has three diagnostics: `exact_checks.py`, `independent_topology.py` and `independent_carrier.py`; the latter two were developed independently and do not import or execute the author's checker. The combined package contains twelve diagnostics, including seven independent implementations; see [the diagnostic guide](../scripts/README.md). Finite tests do not prove the uniform theorem, arbitrary ambient-isotopy injectivity, or PS class minimality. The unrestricted rational PL normaliser and arbitrary parameter-family construction are proved terminating but have not been fully implemented.

No claim is made that the bare contractibility conclusion has not appeared in the literature. The contribution presented here is the explicit marked planar model, the ordinary ambient-isotopy and complete-block class-distance interfaces, and a compatible matching-flow contraction using the stated early PS inputs. No current exhaustive novelty search was performed during preparation of this package. The theorem is not claimed to be formally verified in Lean or certified by a human expert.


## The combined geometric theorem and the splice boundary

Theorem 1.3 concerns the original simultaneous geometric complex on all reduced, componentwise incompressible spanning classes, not just a minimum-genus subcomplex. The Seifert comparison is Theorem 11.2. Finite-system verticalisation retains repeated occurrences and surface labels; independent parallel copies realise every matching on a compatible graph. These facts prove geometric flagness and provide a finite geometric carrier for every closed-simplex cylinder of the deformation.

The splice family is exactly Section 12 and Corollary 21.1. Each product block has at least two actual tori; the permitted single coned cable block can have one. One common torus is the genuine JSJ cut and has nonmatching fibre slopes. The ambient $`S^3`$ link exterior and its actual meridians are separate hypotheses. Section 22 gives actual link witnesses and a fixed-peripheral determinant-only negative control: a unimodular fibre matrix with nonzero fibre intersection can give meridional filling with nontrivial first homology. No general matrix realisation theorem is assumed.

Lemma 13.1 precedes the all-genus minimum-cut Theorem 14.1. Lemma 15.1, Lemma 16.1 and Theorem 16.2 identify ordinary global vertices and absorb the gluing twist by compatible boundary motions and finite return. Lemma 17.1 treats the possibly separating JSJ multicurve directly through PS Proposition 3.3; Theorem 18.1 proves the ordinary global class minimum, with no new extreme indices in the specified horizontal filling. Theorems 19.1 and 20.1 give decoding and finite inclusion nullhomotopies. Appendix A proves the local block inputs.

The geometric comparison does not apply the Seifert all-matching carrier without its missing trace condition. Lemmas 23.1–23.2 normalise a whole disjoint system. Lemma 24.1 identifies a common cyclic order on its horizontal sheets. Local finiteness and countability are not assumed.

## Why the minimum-cut formula is not a JSJ-tree induction

The identity $`\min_{F'\in[F]}\#(F'\cap T)=\mathrm{div}(\Phi|_T)`$ is proved here for the stipulated two-block family only. It fails for general graph-manifold exteriors. For oppositely oriented zero-framed parallels of a nontrivial torus knot, $`\Phi`$ vanishes on the companion exterior and $`\Phi|_T=0`$. A spanning class built from two oppositely oriented companion fibres has two essential, oppositely oriented longitudinal traces on the companion torus; this class cannot be represented off that torus. The annular spanning class, by contrast, has empty trace. The former class is not norm-minimising. The individual essential pieces and their signed trace therefore contain information lost by divisibility. This example explains the scope boundary; no contractibility theorem for that wider family is used in this proof.

More blocks, multiple independently active ports, returning port annuli, blocks without actual boundary, and nonunit actual boundary data are not covered. No statement here promotes an algebraic carrier to a global geometric model without the proved ordinary-isotopy and class-minimum interfaces.

## Review and implementation status of the extension

The Seifert geometric proof received separate geometry and retraction reviews with no fatal or repair-requiring findings. The geometry review's attribution correction is implemented in Section 1 and the bibliography: competition p. 8 states the knot agreement; PS pp. 1490 and 1493 state the disconnected distinction; competition p. 2 identifies the shared-component obstacle. Its returning-arc clarification is implemented in Lemma 10.1: actual boundary curves are disjoint from cutting annuli before the argument, so the nonexistent actual-boundary return branch is removed.

The splice cyclic proof received two adversarial-review rounds and package corrections. The local cone coefficient is $`\beta`$, independent of the gluing entry $`c`$; no extra coprimality is imposed. The splice geometric proof received a fresh-context adversarial review with no fatal or repair-requiring findings and one wording correction. Every low cable singleton assertion is explicitly restricted to the zero side. A one-actual-boundary coned horizontal block need not have demand one or a unique global vertex; it is treated by the horizontal graph and trace-order argument. The independent diagnostic includes a demand-three, six-vertex negative control. Claude completed the final editorial review of this assembled version. The independent alternative derivation of the Seifert comparison is acknowledged without duplicating its proof.

All twelve standard-library diagnostics are reproducible and their reports record package-relative fingerprints. The seven independently developed implementations remain separate. Bounded checks do not prove three-dimensional system normalisation, arbitrary isotopy, the literature inputs or ordinary CW product continuity. The full arbitrary-input PL normaliser and parameter-family procedure are not implemented. Every Markdown page was checked through GitHub's official rendering. No formal verification or human-expert certification is claimed.
