# 23. Simultaneous efficient cuts across the JSJ torus

Use exactly the family and ordinary spanning conventions of Section 12 and Corollary 21.1: an actual oriented non-split $`S^3`$ link exterior with two genuine planar Seifert JSJ blocks, nonmatching fibre slopes, at least two actual boundaries per product block, and at most one retained cone on one side. The coned cable block may have one actual boundary. Being an $`S^3`$ exterior with its stated meridians remains an independent hypothesis.

Put $`k_i=\Phi(h_i)`$ and $`q=\mathrm{div}(\Phi|_T)`$, as in Section 12. The all-genus cut theorem is Theorem 14.1; the ordinary vertex theorem and twist quotient are Section 16; relative horizontal uniqueness is Lemma 15.1; global class distance is Theorem 18.1; the full-region, zero-face-compatible decoder is Theorem 19.1; cyclic contractibility is Theorem 20.1. Corollary 21.1 includes the retained cone. Appendix A proves the block interfaces used by those results. The new comparison adds finite-system normalisation and the trace-order criterion rather than assuming them from a single-vertex normal form.


**Lemma 23.1 (simultaneous efficient cut).** A finite disjoint family $`F_1,\ldots,F_r`$ of spanning representatives can be carried, while retaining disjointness and labels, to a system efficient with respect to the fixed $`T`$. In the mixed branch each member then has exactly $`q`$ coherent traces. In the double-zero branch the entire system misses $`T`$.

**Proof.** Minimise the total number of intersection circles of the **entire embedded union** with $`T`$ under ambient isotopy of that union. The nonnegative integers supply a minimum; no independent motion of the surfaces is being substituted for a common motion.

Inessential circles are removed innermost using componentwise incompressibility, incompressibility of $`T`$ and irreducibility. For the irreducible-ball move, a different component wholly hidden inside its ball would have no actual boundary and would be closed, which is excluded. The usual argument therefore applies to the union. Essential cut pieces inject into their original surfaces and into $`M`$, hence into the blocks. There is no disk or closed cut piece.

A boundary-parallel cut annulus cannot have both ends on an actual torus: that annulus belongs to one $`F_i`$, which uses that actual torus once. Thus its parallelism is to $`T`$. Inside the parallelism solid torus, any other cut piece of any $`F_j`$ has fundamental group injecting into a cyclic group, no disk or closed component, and primitive longitudinal boundary. It is consequently an annulus of the primitive type used in Lemma 13.1. Straighten the finite annulus system by the meridian-disk argument, and choose an innermost empty parallelism. Its torus-side subannulus has no other trace; a sufficiently thin collar on the other side of $`T`$ is also free of the other surfaces there. Pushing across removes two intersection circles without meeting another $`F_j`$. This contradicts total minimality.

Hatcher Lemma 1.11 now gives boundary-incompressibility of every cut piece. We have obtained the same essentiality conclusion as Lemma 13.1 simultaneously. Theorem 14.1 classifies **every efficient representative**, so it applies separately to each member of this still-disjoint system. Its mixed trace count is $`q`$; its double-zero trace is empty. No assumption of unit actual boundary usage for the union, or connectedness of the union complement, has entered. ∎

**Lemma 23.2 (simultaneous block forms).** In the mixed branch a geometric simplex can be represented with the zero-side pieces all vertical and the nonzero-side pieces all horizontal, simultaneously. Repeated parallel components are retained. The trace curves can all be made straight and disjoint on $`T`$.

**Proof.** Apply Lemma 23.1. On each boundary torus of a block, all the relevant trace or actual curves are parallel. A common torus isotopy puts them in the straight slope, and extends over collars. On the zero side this is the fibre slope; on the nonzero side it is transverse to the fibre.

Use the finite-system version of Hatcher's proof of Proposition 1.12, printed pp.20–22. Although the convention on printed p.12 defaults to connected surfaces, the proof extends here as follows. Work with all disjoint pieces together; remove fibre neighbourhoods and cut the circle bundle to a solid torus. Choose innermost circles and edgemost arcs relative to the entire collection. Every disk supplied by incompressibility lies on the one original component containing its boundary. In the disk-cleaning step of printed p.21, cleaning against all other cut pieces, or reducing the cut traces when that disk leaves the solid torus, therefore works for this finite collection just as for one surface. Boundary-compressing alternatives are excluded by Lemma 23.1. The moves are isotopies of the embedded collection, not surgeries or deletions of original components.

Disjoint essential curves of the two different types cannot coexist on the boundary torus of the cut solid torus. The actual boundary slope fixes the type. In the zero block the cut pieces are the finite nested boundary-parallel vertical annuli, straightened relative to their boundaries. In the nonzero block they are meridian disks, straightened together by the disjoint-disk isotopy in the ball obtained by a meridian cut. These are the two final branches of Hatcher's proof on printed p.22. They glue along their fixed cut-face boundaries. In particular the isotopies can fix the already straight collared $`T`$ trace.

This proof also includes a retained exceptional fibre: the deleted neighbourhood and its meridian-disk patches are those in the same printed proof. In a zero block no horizontal patch remains; in a nonzero block the horizontal disks are put in their simultaneous standard position. All component labels, grouping and orientations are carried along. On a coned zero side keep its cone neighbourhood marked and unused, as in Section 21. ∎

It follows that the union of the component arc classes of a geometric simplex has one noncrossing proper support graph $`G`$ on the ordinary zero-side base. Straighten the embedded multiarc system with multiplicities, retaining its cyclic endpoint order and component labels, and collapse the parallel occurrences only when recording the distinct classes of $`G`$. Thus its trace word is an allocation of copies inside the incident edge bundles of this same graph. The ordinary geodesic/bigon criterion [Farb–Margalit](../sources/bibliography.md#ordinary-surface-topology), printed pp.33–36, provides the canonical distinct representatives. This proves the common-graph part, but not yet the simultaneous horizontal assembly of all its matchings.
